Expression Plasticity

Coefficient of Variation Matrices

Summary of Available Data

The coefficient of variation (CV) of exon expression per gene provides a measure of expression plasticity. Higher CV values indicate greater variability in exon usage across samples, which may reflect alternative splicing or differential exon utilization under varying conditions.

About the Data

  • Exon CV
  • Exon Count Matrix

The coefficient of variation (CV) matrix represents the variability of exon expression within each gene across samples. For each gene, the CV is calculated as the standard deviation of exon-level expression divided by the mean, providing a normalized measure of expression variability. This metric captures expression plasticity at the exon level, which may reflect:

  • Alternative splicing events: Genes with high CV may utilize different exons under different conditions
  • Differential exon usage: Environmental or developmental changes may shift which exons are predominantly expressed
  • Regulatory complexity: Higher CV values may indicate more complex post-transcriptional regulation

Genes with consistently low CV values show stable exon usage patterns, while those with high CV demonstrate greater plasticity in their transcript isoform composition.

The exon count matrix provides raw expression counts at the exon level for each gene across all samples. This data enables:

  • Differential exon usage analysis: Compare exon expression patterns between conditions or time points
  • Alternative splicing detection: Identify genes with variable exon inclusion/exclusion patterns
  • Isoform-level expression: Understand transcript diversity within genes
  • Foundation for CV calculation: The exon counts serve as the basis for computing the coefficient of variation metrics

Each row represents a unique exon-gene combination, with columns containing sample-specific read counts. This granular data supports detailed investigation of post-transcriptional regulation mechanisms.
